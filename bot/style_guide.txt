STYLE LESSONS FROM THE TOP MEME PAGES IN THIS NICHE (Paul's reference posts). Copy the craft, never the content:
- Very short: most captions are 4-15 words. One idea, zero explanation.
- The photo does half the joke: an absurd, candid or deadpan photo (a baby wearing a huge backpack, a guy in a voting booth, a serious man at a chess table, a smug close-up face) with a caption that re-frames it. Contrast between caption and photo is the engine.
- "How [person] looks/feels when/after [ultra-specific situation]" works well with ONE vivid concrete detail (an object, a place, a weird habit, an age, a brand used harmlessly). Clock times and money amounts are a crutch: at most one meme in ten should use one.
- Ironic twist captions: a motivational/normal statement over a photo that contradicts it ("find a job you love..." over a wildly grinning person).
- Observation captions: a tiny weird real-life thing said deadpan ("Holy Bonny blue" over one odd bottle among a shelf of identical ones). "first day at the ___" over a photo of someone/something visibly overwhelmed.
- Snapchat-style caption: plain white text in a dark translucent band across the photo is as effective as a top bar.
- Everyday targets that land: school/teachers, jobs and customers, siblings/parents, dating and exes, gym, roommates, pets, your own body betraying you, bad decisions. Rotate widely; a page that keeps making the same joke dies.
- These pages are EDGY and that is part of why they perform: dark humor, crude and suggestive jokes, innuendo and double meanings, cringe, "bro WHAT" shock-then-share energy. Match that energy, aimed at yourself, a situation, or a generic type of person.
- Hard lines that get pages banned (do not cross): slurs, jokes demeaning a group (race, religion, sexuality, gender identity, disability, nationality), explicit sexual description, mocking a real identifiable person's looks/weight/hairline, real names (celebrities, politicians, strangers), political candidates or parties, glorifying violence or self-harm.

STYLE LESSONS FROM THE TOP MEME PAGES IN THIS NICHE (Paul's reference posts). Copy the craft, never the content:
- Very short: most captions are 4-15 words. One idea, zero explanation.
- The photo does half the joke: an absurd, candid or deadpan photo (a baby wearing a huge backpack, a guy in a voting booth, a serious man at a chess table, a smug close-up face) with a caption that re-frames it. Contrast between caption and photo is the engine.
- "How [person] looks/feels when/after [ultra-specific situation]" works very well, with an exact detail: a clock time (8:24 AM), a duration (5 minutes into a 6am shift), a number, a brand name used harmlessly (Starbucks), an age (14 year old).
- Ironic twist captions: a motivational/normal statement over a photo that contradicts it ("find a job you love..." over a wildly grinning person).
- Observation captions: a tiny weird real-life thing said deadpan ("Holy Bonny blue" over one odd bottle among a shelf of identical ones). "first day at the ___" over a photo of someone/something visibly overwhelmed.
- Snapchat-style caption: plain white text in a dark translucent band across the photo is as effective as a top bar.
- Everyday targets that always land: school/teachers, 6am shifts and coffee shops, sleep, being broke, group chats, siblings/parents, gym, waiting for replies.
- AVOID what those pages do that could get an account restricted: sexual or crude jokes, race jokes, body or hairline shaming, real named people, politics/elections, pronoun or identity jokes. Get the same laugh with a harmless target (yourself, a situation, school, work, food).
